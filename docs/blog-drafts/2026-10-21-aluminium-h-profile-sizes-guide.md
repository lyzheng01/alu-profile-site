# Aluminium H Profile Sizes: What to Measure Before You Request a Quote

An aluminium H profile is often chosen to align or join panels, but “H profile” alone does not define a usable size. The cross-section, open spaces, wall thicknesses and finished surface all affect whether the mating parts fit.

There is no single size chart that applies to every supplier or H-shaped section. Start with a section drawing and the parts the profile must fit. Treat the symbols below as a clear enquiry notation, not a universal industry-wide naming system.

## Show the full cross-section, not just “H profile”

Draw the profile in the orientation used by the assembly and mark the functional faces. This avoids confusion when a section is asymmetric, rotated in use or sold under different names such as H section or H channel.

![H-section dimension diagram showing overall width W, overall height H, side-leg thickness tf, web thickness tw and clear openings g1 and g2](/images/aluminium-h-profile-dimensions.svg)
*Dimension reference only. The symbols are used for this guide and do not imply nominal values or tolerances.*

Include the dimensions that define your part:

- **W — overall width:** outside-to-outside across the two side legs in the drawing view.
- **H — overall height:** outside-to-outside in the perpendicular direction. State the orientation so W and H cannot be swapped.
- **tw — web thickness:** thickness of the central connecting web.
- **tf — side-leg thickness:** thickness of each outer leg. If the two legs differ, call them out separately.
- **g1 and g2 — clear openings:** usable gaps on either side of the web where panels or adjoining parts fit. Specify each opening if they are not equal, and identify the height or location where it matters.
- **L — cut length:** finished length along the extrusion direction, with end squareness, deburring, holes or machining noted as needed.

![A digital caliper checking the central web of an aluminium H-section extrusion](/images/aluminium-h-profile-web-thickness-measurement.webp)

A catalogue may use different letters or combine dimensions. Copy the supplier’s current drawing when selecting an existing section; for a custom profile, define each critical face and dimension directly on your own drawing.

## Match both openings to the parts being joined

Measure the mating panel or insert, then compare it with the required clear opening at the actual engagement depth. Include assembly clearance, any gasket or liner, and the effect of the selected finish. If the two sides of the H section serve different parts, specify g1 and g2 independently rather than assuming symmetry.

For a sliding or removable panel, state the acceptable minimum and maximum opening if known. For a fixed joint, identify the required fit and how it will be checked. If the design is not frozen, provide the mating-part drawing and mark which dimensions are fixed and which the supplier may propose.

## Specify tolerances at the functional faces

A general note such as “standard tolerance” may not control the fit you need. Identify the critical dimensions, applicable standard and revision, and inspection condition. State whether dimensions apply before or after anodizing, coating or another finish.

The Aluminum Association’s extrusion-tolerance guide distinguishes metal dimensions from space dimensions; clear openings can be space dimensions. The applicable tolerance still depends on the adopted standard, profile and agreement with the producer. See the [Aluminum Association’s guide to extrusion tolerances](https://www.aluminum.org/understanding-aluminum-extrusion-tolerances) and LingYe’s [aluminium extrusion drawing tolerance guide](/en/news/aluminum-extrusion-drawing-tolerances).

## Complete the material and processing notes

Specify alloy and temper, surface finish and colour, visible faces, cut length, secondary machining, quantity and packaging. Do not infer strength or load capacity from H shape or wall thickness alone; an application with structural loads needs a qualified engineering check using the full section, support conditions, connections and load case.

## Copy-ready H profile enquiry checklist

Send the supplier a section drawing and include:

1. Overall W and H, with the drawing orientation shown.
2. Web thickness tw and each side-leg thickness tf.
3. Clear openings g1 and g2 at the fit location, including acceptable limits if known.
4. Mating panel or component dimensions and required clearance.
5. Alloy, temper, finish and whether dimensions are checked before or after finishing.
6. Cut length L, end and machining requirements, quantity and drawing revision.
7. Applicable tolerance standard and the specific dimensions that control fit.

**Short specification line:** H profile: W ___ × H ___; web tw ___; side legs tf ___ / ___; openings g1 ___ and g2 ___; length L ___; alloy/temper ___; finish ___; critical tolerances ___; drawing revision ___. Fill the blanks from the project drawing; this template is not a standard size chart.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are aluminium H profiles available in standard sizes?
Some suppliers list existing sections, while other projects require a drawing-based or custom profile. Check the supplier’s current section drawing before assuming a size, tolerance or die is available.

### Do both openings have to be the same?
No. Define g1 and g2 separately if the section is asymmetric or the two sides serve different parts.

### Does a thicker web mean the profile can carry more load?
Not by itself. Capacity depends on the complete section, alloy and temper, supports, connections and load case; obtain an appropriate engineering assessment for structural use.

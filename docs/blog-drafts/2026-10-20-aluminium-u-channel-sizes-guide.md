People searching for aluminium U channel sizes usually need more than a nominal width. They need to know whether the channel will fit a panel, guide, edge or adjoining part, and what dimensions to put on a drawing or RFQ.

There is no single size chart that suits every U channel. The dimensions depend on the required fit, section geometry, alloy and temper, finish, length and tolerance. LingYe’s U-channel listing identifies the specification as project-specific and asks buyers to confirm it by drawing or specification. Ask the supplier whether a suitable existing section is available before assuming a custom size or tooling is required.

## The dimensions to put on a U-channel drawing

Use a section view and define the measurement points. The letters below are a simple notation for an enquiry, not a required industry-wide symbol system.

![U-channel schematic with outside base width B, overall leg height H, wall thickness T and clear opening W marked](/images/aluminium-u-channel-section-dimensions.svg)
*Dimension reference only. No nominal size or tolerance is implied.*

- **B — outside base width:** the overall width across the outside faces of the base.
- **H — overall leg height:** the outside height from the base underside to the top of each leg. If the legs differ, specify H1 and H2 separately.
- **T — wall thickness:** state whether the base and both legs share one nominal thickness. Call out each wall separately if they do not.
- **W — clear opening:** the usable inside width between the legs, measured at the height where the mating part must fit.
- **L — cut length:** the finished length along the extrusion direction. Also state whether ends must be square, deburred, drilled or machined.

![Digital caliper checking the clear opening of an aluminium U channel](/images/aluminium-u-channel-opening-measurement.webp)
*Illustrative inspection setup. Confirm functional dimensions and tolerance on the approved drawing.*


If B and T are outside dimensions and the walls are uniform, `B − 2T` can be a first estimate of the opening. It is not a substitute for dimensioning W: corner radii, taper, wall variation and the height of the fit can change the usable clearance. Dimension the functional opening directly on the drawing.

## Choose the fit before choosing a nominal size

Start with the part the channel must hold, guide or join. Record its measured width or thickness, the required running or assembly clearance, and the point along the channel where the fit matters. Include any gasket, coating, liner or fastener that occupies space. A bare-metal opening can become a different fit after finishing or assembly.

For a sliding or guided part, specify the minimum and maximum opening that the assembly can accept, if known. For an edge cap or panel retainer, show the panel thickness and how much engagement is required. If dimensions are still being developed, send the mating-part drawing and clearly mark which dimensions are fixed and which may be proposed.

## Wall thickness does not determine load capacity by itself

A thicker wall may change stiffness and weight, but it does not establish a safe load rating on its own. Alloy and temper, leg height, base width, support spacing, load direction, connection method and allowable deflection all matter. If the U channel supports a structural or safety-critical load, provide the load case and support conditions and have the design checked by a qualified engineer. Do not select a section from a size chart alone.

## Specify tolerances and finish for the actual interfaces

Do not write only “standard tolerance” if the opening controls fit. Identify the applicable drawing standard and edition, the dimensions that are critical, and the acceptance method. The Aluminum Association’s tolerance guidance distinguishes metal dimensions from space dimensions; its examples include a channel opening as a space dimension. The tolerance that applies still depends on the governing specification, profile and project agreement. See the [Aluminum Association’s extrusion tolerance guide](https://www.aluminum.org/understanding-aluminum-extrusion-tolerances) and LingYe’s guide to [marking tolerances on an extrusion drawing](/en/news/aluminum-extrusion-drawing-tolerances).

Also state the required alloy and temper, surface treatment, colour or appearance standard, and whether dimensions are measured before or after finishing. Confirm the intended standard section, if any, against the current supplier drawing; catalogue names alone do not define a mating fit.

## Copy-ready U-channel RFQ checklist

Include the following with your enquiry:

1. A dimensioned cross-section showing B, H, T and the functional clear opening W.
2. Finished cut length L, end condition and any drilling, slots or machining.
3. The mating part, required engagement and fit or clearance limits.
4. Alloy, temper, surface treatment and visible-face requirements, if specified.
5. Critical dimensions, applicable tolerance standard and drawing revision.
6. Quantity, intended application, destination and packaging requirements.
7. Whether an existing die or standard section may be proposed, or whether the section must match the supplied drawing.

A useful line-item format is: **B ___ × H ___ × T ___; clear opening W ___; cut length L ___; alloy/temper ___; finish ___; critical tolerances ___; drawing revision ___**. Fill in the values from your project drawing rather than treating the blanks as standard catalogue sizes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are aluminium U channels available in standard sizes?
Some suppliers may offer standard sections, while other projects need a section selected or developed from a drawing. Ask which current profile drawing and tolerance apply to the quoted section.

### Can I choose a channel by its outside width alone?
Usually that is not enough when the channel has to fit another part. Specify the clear opening, wall thickness, leg height and the location where the fit is checked.

### Does a larger wall thickness always make the channel stronger?
No single dimension determines strength. The full section, alloy and temper, span, support and load direction need to be considered together.

When you request an aluminium extrusion quote, the overall dimensions alone do not tell you how many kilograms of metal are in each metre. For a useful estimate, you need the profile’s **actual cross-sectional area** and the **density used for the specified alloy**.

This guide shows the calculation, explains what the result includes and lists the information to confirm with your supplier. The numbers below are an illustrative calculation, not a LingYe catalogue section or a delivery guarantee.

## Start with the aluminium area, not the outside box

In the formula, **A** is the area of aluminium in one cross-section, measured in square millimetres (mm²). For a hollow profile, count the metal walls and exclude the open spaces. A profile’s outside width multiplied by its outside height gives a bounding rectangle, not the amount of aluminium in the section.

![Hollow extrusion cross-section with the metal walls highlighted and the internal void excluded](/images/aluminium-extrusion-cross-section-area.svg)
*Illustrative section only. Use the area from the approved drawing, CAD model or supplier data.*

For a custom or complex profile, use the area calculated from the released CAD drawing or ask the extruder for its mass-per-length data. Small changes to wall thickness, corner radii or the approved section can change the area. The Aluminum Extruders Council also notes that profile area and weight-per-foot are among the design considerations related to extrusion press selection; the producer should review the complete section for manufacturability.

## The formula for theoretical weight per metre

If the area is in mm² and density is in g/cm³, use:

**Theoretical weight (kg/m) = A (mm²) × density (g/cm³) ÷ 1,000**

The division by 1,000 converts the units to kilograms per metre. Use the density for the specified alloy rather than assuming every aluminium alloy has exactly the same value. The Aluminum Association’s [Teal Sheets](https://www.aluminum.org/tealsheets) publish nominal densities by alloy designation; use the value required by your project or confirmed by the supplier.

![Formula for theoretical aluminium extrusion weight per metre using cross-sectional area and material density](/images/aluminium-extrusion-weight-formula.svg)
*A is the true aluminium area of the section; ρ is the density used for the specified alloy.*

### Worked example

Assume an illustrative section area of **320 mm²** and a density of **2.70 g/cm³**:

**320 × 2.70 ÷ 1,000 = 0.864 kg/m**

The result is a theoretical linear weight for this example. It is not a published stock size, a supplier’s final mass or an agreed tolerance.

## Convert kg/m to one cut length or an order

Multiply linear weight by the cut length in metres to estimate the theoretical metal weight of one piece. Multiply that result by the number of pieces for a theoretical order total.

Using the example above, a 6 m length would be **0.864 kg/m × 6 m = 5.184 kg per piece**. An order of 100 such pieces would have an estimated profile-only net weight of **518.4 kg**. These figures exclude packaging and should not be used as a carrier’s gross shipment weight.

![Diagram showing linear weight multiplied by cut length to estimate theoretical net weight per piece](/images/aluminium-extrusion-weight-per-piece.svg)

## Why the delivered weight can differ

The calculation is only as accurate as its inputs. Confirm the drawing revision and section area, alloy and temper, cut-length basis, and the supplier’s method for stating or accepting weight. Production dimensions, the exact section geometry, finish, cut pieces and weighing method can affect the result. The acceptable limits depend on the governing specification and the agreement for the order; do not assume a universal weight tolerance.

Keep **profile net weight** separate from **packaging tare** and **shipment gross weight**. If the estimate is for freight, ask which weight the carrier needs and how the bundle, pallet, wrapping and straps are included.

## What to include in a weight-related RFQ

Send the supplier:

1. The approved cross-section drawing or CAD file, including its revision.
2. The calculated section area or the supplier’s quoted kg/m, and which source controls.
3. Alloy and temper, plus any surface-finish requirements.
4. Cut length, quantity and whether the quantity is specified by pieces, metres or kilograms.
5. The agreed method and limits for dimensional or weight acceptance.
6. Whether you need profile net weight, packed gross weight or both.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I calculate the weight of a hollow aluminium profile?

Yes. Use the area of aluminium material in the section, excluding the hollow spaces. For a complex shape, use CAD area or supplier data instead of estimating from the outside dimensions.

### Does kg/m give the shipping weight?

No. It estimates the profile’s theoretical metal weight per metre. Packaging, pallets and other shipment materials add tare weight; confirm the gross shipment weight separately.

### Is the calculated value the guaranteed production weight?

No. It is an estimate based on the section area and density entered. Agree the applicable drawing, specification and weight-acceptance method with the supplier.

### Why does the extruder ask for profile weight?

Weight per length is one design and production consideration. The Aluminum Extruders Council’s [extrusion design guidance](https://members.aec.org/page/extrusion-design-considerations) discusses profile area and weight-per-foot alongside press-size considerations. The producer must review the full profile and production conditions.

**Technical note:** This guide explains a theoretical mass estimate. It does not replace a released drawing, supplier calculation, order specification or freight weighing record.

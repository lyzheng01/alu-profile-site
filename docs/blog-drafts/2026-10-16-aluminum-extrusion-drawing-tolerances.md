An extrusion drawing can show the shape without saying how much variation is acceptable. The quickest way to make a quote useful is to separate three different checks: **cross-section dimensions**, **straightness along the length**, and **twist around the length**. They describe different features and need different inspection setups.

## 1. Cross-section dimensions: will it fit?

Mark the dimensions that connect to another part: overall width and height, wall thickness, slot opening, cavity size, or distance between mounting faces. Identify the datum and the measurement points. A solid outside width and an open slot are different dimension types, so do not assume the same tolerance rule applies to both.

![Annotated aluminum profile cross-section showing overall width, height, slot opening and datum face](/images/extrusion-fit-dimensions.svg)

## 2. Straightness: does it bow along the length?

State the length or inspection span over which straightness matters. A long supplied bar and a short cut piece can behave differently in an assembly. Tell the supplier whether the check applies to the full bar, each cut piece, or both.

## 3. Twist: does the section rotate along the length?

Twist is rotation of the cross-section around the profile axis. It is separate from bow: a profile can be straight yet twisted. Show the reference face and agree on the support and measurement method for any critical requirement.

![Separate inspection setups for profile straightness and twist over a stated measurement length](/images/extrusion-straightness-twist-measurement.svg)

## Copy this into your RFQ

> Please quote drawing **[number / revision]**, alloy and temper **[ ]**, finish **[ ]**, cut length **[ ]**, quantity **[ ]**. Apply **[named standard and edition]** to dimensions not individually controlled. Critical fit features: **[feature + tolerance + datum]**. Straightness: **[limit] over [inspection length]**. Twist: **[limit] over [inspection length]**, measured from **[reference face]**. Inspect critical dimensions **[before/after finishing]**. Please list any assumptions or deviations and confirm capability before production.

Leave the brackets blank until the design owner confirms the functional need. There is no single tolerance value that fits every profile. The applicable standard, geometry, dimension type, alloy/temper, length, finish and inspection method all matter. Ask the extruder to confirm the selected requirement for the actual cross-section; do not copy a value from an unrelated profile.

## Before sending the drawing

- Name the dimensional standard and edition; avoid “standard tolerance” without a reference.
- Mark only dimensions that control fit, sealing, alignment or load transfer as critical.
- Give machined holes and faces their own datums and tolerances.
- State whether critical dimensions are checked before or after coating.
- Agree on the measurement setup and first-article report for critical features.

### What tolerance should I use?

There is no universal number. Specify the applicable standard and the function of each critical feature, then confirm capability with the extruder. The Aluminum Extruders Council explains why tolerance tables depend on the applicable standard and part details in its [tolerance overview](https://aec.org/tolerances).

### Are extrusion and CNC tolerances the same?

No. Extrusion creates the continuous cross-section; machining creates local features such as holes and precision faces. State and inspect these requirements separately.
